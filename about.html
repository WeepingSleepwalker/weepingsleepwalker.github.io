Bio

Francis Chung is a Dallas-based sculptor and art critic. He makes hand-built ceramic figures that look composed and are searching for something that cannot be found. He holds an MFA in Sculpture from Cranbrook Academy of Art (2019) and has completed residencies at Vermont Studio Center (2018) and the School of Visual Arts (2016). He writes art criticism and interviews for Glasstire.

Statement

A child shields his eyes against a light he can't see, because his eyes are shut. He is looking for something that cannot be found, and so is everyone else here. A devil throws back his head and howls at nothing in particular. A cat sits with its lids half closed, past expecting an answer. A figure squats with a cigarette, cool and empty, his eyes two holes. Saturn, wide-eyed and bloody-mouthed, devours his son. Even the superhero looks worn out by the search.

From a distance they look composed. Up close the seams show.

The surfaces stay rough and the glazes run wet: a yellow like a warning light, a blue gone dark, a black that holds the glare. Where the mood drops, the surface goes matte. I set the figures on stone and wood, so that a longing lasting seconds rests on material that has lasted millennia.
