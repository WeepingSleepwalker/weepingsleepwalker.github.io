# Contest-Ready Artist Website Draft

This is a lean website plan designed to support a contest submission where the form asks for **socials and website**. The goal is not to build a huge portfolio site. The goal is to make you look serious, coherent, and easy to evaluate.

---

## Core principle

The site should say:

**artist first, writer second**

Your writing can strengthen the work, but it should not compete with it.

The overall vibe should be:

- minimal
- edited
- visually clean
- conceptually serious
- no clutter
- no weak or old work

---

## Recommended sitemap

Use a very simple structure:

1. **Home**
2. **Work**
3. **About**
4. **Writing**
5. **Contact**

If you want to go even leaner, you can combine **About** and **Contact**.

---

## What each page should do

### Home
Purpose: immediate impression.

Include:
- your name
- one strong hero image
- one short line describing the practice
- simple nav

Suggested homepage line options:

**Option 1**  
Sculptural work exploring fragmentation, form, ritual, and structural tension.

**Option 2**  
I make sculptural objects and installations that move between fragmentation, architecture, and devotional intensity.

**Option 3**  
Artist based in Dallas, working across sculpture, installation, and writing.

Keep the homepage very sparse.

---

### Work
Purpose: prove the practice.

Include:
- 6–10 of your strongest works
- title
- year
- materials
- dimensions
- 1–3 images per work if available
- optional short caption only if it helps

Do **not** overload with too many projects.

What to prioritize:
- work closest in spirit to the contest submission
- work that feels formally resolved
- work that shows ambition or a distinct voice

What to leave out:
- experiments that are not visually convincing
- older work that does not fit the current direction
- anything that makes the practice feel split or inconsistent

Suggested section intro:

> A selection of recent sculptural works and installations.

---

### About
Purpose: establish seriousness and context.

Keep this short. Around 100–180 words is enough.

## Draft short bio

**Version A**  
Francis Chung is a Dallas-based artist whose work explores fragmentation, structure, devotion, and psychological intensity through sculpture and installation. His practice is shaped by an interest in architecture, ritual space, symbolic form, and the tension between rupture and coherence. Alongside his studio work, he writes on contemporary art, using criticism as a parallel way of thinking through form, meaning, and cultural context.

**Version B**  
Francis Chung is a Dallas-based artist working primarily in sculpture and installation. His work often centers on fragmentation, structural force, and devotional or symbolic charge, drawing from architecture, religious imagery, and the psychological space of objects. He also writes on contemporary art, a practice that sharpens and extends his engagement with form, intention, and interpretation.

## Draft artist statement

Use a short statement, not a manifesto.

**Version A**  
My work is driven by fragmentation: how forms break apart, hold together, and carry emotional or symbolic weight. I am interested in sculpture as a site where structure and vulnerability meet, and where objects can feel devotional, architectural, or psychologically charged at the same time. Across materials and formats, I want the work to hold tension between clarity and disturbance, intimacy and monumentality.

**Version B**  
I approach sculpture as a way of building pressure into form. Fragmentation, architectural structure, and symbolic resonance recur throughout the work, not as themes illustrated in advance, but as forces that emerge through making. I am drawn to objects that feel unstable but deliberate, devotional yet unresolved, intimate while reaching toward something monumental.

### Writing
Purpose: support the identity without overpowering the site.

This page should be selective.

Include:
- 3–6 of your strongest published pieces
- title
- publication
- year
- short one-line descriptor if useful
- external links

Suggested intro:

> Selected writing on contemporary art, exhibition-making, and artistic practice.

Do not dump every article here.

If the writing is still growing, you can even frame it as:

> Selected criticism and interviews.

That sounds more intentional than a giant archive.

- Dallas, TX

## Best ordering for the Work page

Lead with the strongest image first, not necessarily the most recent work.

A good sequence is:

1. strongest / most iconic piece
2. most contest-relevant piece
3. piece showing range
4. piece showing material intelligence
5. piece showing conceptual depth
6. anything else that strengthens the overall arc

Think of the Work page like hanging a small show.

---

## What jurors should feel after 30 seconds

After clicking your site, they should think:

- this is a real practice
- this person edits themselves
- the work has a coherent voice
- the writing deepens the work rather than distracting from it
- this artist is worth taking seriously

---

## What to avoid

Avoid all of this:

- long autobiographical homepage text
- too many categories
- too many unfinished works
- random old projects
- weak installation photos
- too much theory language
- black backgrounds unless done very well
- cluttered menus
- pages still saying lorem ipsum or coming soon
- a writing section larger than the art section

Also avoid sounding like you are trying to impress with references.

Better: let the work carry the ambition.

---

## Fast build strategy

If you need this up quickly, do this:

### Minimum viable site
- Home
- Work
- About
- Contact

Then add **Writing** only if it looks strong enough.

### Build order
1. choose template
2. upload hero image
3. upload 6–10 works
4. paste short bio
5. paste short statement
6. add contact + Instagram
7. add writing links if they strengthen the package
8. test on phone and desktop
9. submit website

---

## Platform recommendation

If speed matters most:
- Squarespace
- Adobe Portfolio
- Cargo
- Framer

If you want control and already use VS Code:
- a very simple static site in HTML/CSS
- Astro or Next.js only if you already know the workflow

For this contest, speed and polish matter more than technical complexity.

## Image guidance

Use:
- bright, neutral background when possible
- consistent crop logic
- no tiny images
- no blurry process shots unless they are excellent
- no screenshots from Instagram if you can avoid it

If the photos vary in quality, use fewer works.

A tight six is better than a muddy twelve.

---

## Final recommendation

Because the submission explicitly asks for **socials and website**, you should absolutely give them a clean site, even if it is simple.

The strongest version is not a giant archive. It is a controlled, contest-adjacent presentation of your best work that says:

**this artist already has a practice**

That is the win.

---

## One-sentence test

Before publishing, ask:

**Does this site make me look more focused than my Instagram does?**

If yes, it is doing its job.
